Tourism

Two Journeys, One City

Buxar is best approached the way its own history moves — along the water. On one side of the touristic map stand the ghats and temples of the Ramayana: the line Rama drew, the village where Ahilya came back to the world, the Shiva temple of Brahmapur, and the five paths of the Panchkoshi. On the other side rise the monuments of a harder story — the field of the Battle of Buxar, the museum on the ghat, the town park by the railway, and the Chausa Historical Site where Sher Shah Suri turned the tide of an empire.

Every destination on this page is documented from published sources — district records, census data and historical accounts.

Panchkoshi Journey

The Five Sacred Paths

Local tradition records five sacred routes — panch-koshi — linked to the Rama journey through this country; a five-day mela and an annual parikrama keep them alive.

One of the most distinctive local celebrations, the Panchkoshi Mela maps Rama’s voyage across five ceremonial paths, one honoured on each of the festival’s five days. Devotees also walk a Panch Kosi Parikrama of the region, with the largest gatherings reported in the Margashirsha month, when the townlands around Ahirauli fill with pilgrims.

Travel Information

How to Experience Buxar

Practical guidance for your journey — how to reach Buxar, getting around, and the best season to visit.

By Rail & Road

Buxar stands on the main Patna–Varanasi railway line; the station lies within the town, minutes from the ghats. The town sits on the road corridor between the two cities — daily trains and buses serve the district.

Getting Around

The ghats, the museum and Kamal Dah Park are walkable from the station. Auto-rickshaws ply the town; for Ahirauli, Brahmeshwar Nath and Chausa, hire an auto or taxi from the station area and set your fare in advance.

When to Visit & Etiquette

October to March offers the easiest travel; Makar Sankranti and Chhath bring the great crowds to the ghats. At temples, dress modestly and remove footwear at the threshold; at ceremonies, stay quiet and seek permission before photographing people.

Timings and entry details are subject to change. Please confirm current schedules locally before travelling.

Spiritual Site

Ram Rekha Ghat

Ganga riverfront, Buxar town

The ghat is Ram Rekha — “Rama’s line” — the river crossed by Rama, Lakshmana and sage Vishwamitra on the road to Janakpur, and believed to bear the mark of Rama’s feet.

According to the local Ramayana tradition, Rama paused at the river and drew a line across the waters with his bow so the party could pass; the ghat that grew there keeps his name. Pilgrims bathe here through the year, and the town gathers in force for Makar Sankranti (Khichri) and Chhath, when the emptied river beds become wrestling arenas. It is the ceremonial heart of a town some still call a laghu Kashi — where the Ganga runs north, as it does at Varanasi.

Best Time
October–March; Makar Sankranti & Chhath for the fairs
Entry
Public ghat — open; no entry fee published

On the main ghat front of Buxar town.

Spiritual Site

Ahirauli — Devi Ahilya Temple

Ahirauli village, Buxar district (~5 km north-east of town, reported)

A quiet village dham where — according to local tradition — the curse of sage Gautam was lifted, and Ahilya, restored by the touch of Rama’s feet, was born again to the world.

In the Ramayana, Ahilya waits as stone until Rama’s footfall restores her. Near Buxar, tradition sets the memory of that restoration in the village of Ahirauli, a name said to grow out of Ahilya’s own. The Devi Ahilya temple here is described in district literature as dating — “according to local tradition” — back to prehistoric ages, and the village forms a recognised stop of the Panch Kosi Parikrama of Buxar. You come for the legend, and for the calm that makes it believable.

Best Time
October–March
Setting
Village temple — carries the Panch Kosi Parikrama

Village setting; carried in the annual Panch Kosi Parikrama of Buxar.

Spiritual Site

Baba Brahmeshwar Nath Temple

Brahmapur area, Buxar district (about 38 km from town by road, per visitors’ reports)

A Shiva temple of Brahmapur — distant enough to keep its silence, near enough to be reached by a season of walking devotees.

Baba Brahmeshwar Nath is counted among the most visited shrines of the district. Through the month of Shravan, devotees of Shiva are reported walking the road from Ram Rekha Ghat to the temple to make their offerings. A persistent local account also associates the poet-saint Tulsidas with this shrine, though that link belongs to tradition rather than documented record. Temple etiquette is simple and devout dress, and always remove footwear at the threshold.

Best Time
Shravan (Jul–Aug) for the walking season; Oct–Mar for travel ease
Tradition
Tulsidas association is local tradition, not verified record

Brahmapur station/area, Buxar district. Confirm the route locally.

Historic Site · 1764

Battle Ground of Buxar, 1764

On the Ganga near Buxar Fort

Here, on 22–23 October 1764, the East India Company — in the person of Major Hector Munro — broke the combined host of the emperor and the nawabs, and with it the old order of the subcontinent.

The troops of Shah Alam II, Mir Qasim and Shuja-ud-Daula gathered beneath the shadow of Buxar Fort, close to forty thousand strong. Munro’s seven thousand — principally Indian sepoys of the Company — held the line on the 22nd and broke the allied centre on the 23rd. The Company listed 289 killed, 499 wounded and 85 missing; the allies lost over two thousand men. Within a year the Treaty of Allahabad (1765) delivered the diwani — the revenue administration — of Bengal, Bihar and Orissa into Company hands. No army of the old alliance would ever again stand on one field.

Date
22–23 October 1764
Forces
≈40,000 allied vs under 7,000 Company
Outcome
Company victory; Treaty of Allahabad 1765

On the Ganga near Buxar Fort — the river face of the battle.

Museum

Sitaram Upadhyaya Memorial Museum

Ram Rekha Ghat, Buxar

A compact archaeological museum on the ghat, assembled from the lifetime collection of scholar Sitaram Upadhyay — terracottas, coins and relics of the Buxar country.

The museum preserves terracotta figurines, ancient coins and antiquities said to have been recovered from around Buxar Fort, some believed to date back close to two millennia. Its coin cabinet has in recent years held Gupta-era gold coins found in the area and brought here for safekeeping. It is a small, specialist institution rather than a grand gallery — of a piece with the quiet dignity of the ghat it stands on.

Reported Hours
10.30 am – 4.00 pm · closed Mondays (reported — confirm before visiting)
Entry
Not published — confirm at the museum

On Ram Rekha Ghat itself, a short walk from the bathing steps.

Park

Kamal Dah Park

Station Road (Civil Lines), Buxar

A green pause in the middle of the bazaar — a public park around an old town pond, close to the railway station that gave it its address.

Kamal Dah Park sits on Station Road in Civil Lines, a few minutes from Buxar Railway Station. Local visitors use the park for morning walks around the water and treat it as a small historic pocket of the town. The old pond traditions of this corner of Buxar — sometimes linked with the town-name arguments around VyaghraSar — make a walk here a walk through local memory. Keep expectations modest: it is a neighbourhood park, not a landscaped garden.

Best Time
Early morning or late evening
Entry
Public park — reported free, confirm locally

About 0.2 km from Buxar Railway Station, on Station Road.

Historical Site · 1539

Chausa Historical Site

Chausa, about 10 miles south-west of Buxar

The riverside where, on 26 June 1539, Sher Shah Suri broke Humayun’s Mughal army and forged the Sur century — and a place remembered, in peace, for mangoes.

At the flooded junction of the Karamnasa with the Ganga, the Sur dynasty routed the Mughal host. Sher Shah’s Ujjainiya Rajput allies took the waterside; the Mughal line scattered, thousands perished in the waters, and the camp, the artillery and the imperial ladies fell — the harem treated, by order, with honour. Today the area is a quiet historical countryside, known as well for its mango gardens. We call it the Chausa Historical Site: the precise boundaries of the battlefield are part of local heritage, and we do not claim a standing fortress.

Date
26 June 1539
Location
Chausa, on the Karamnasa-Ganga floodplain
Outcome
Decisive victory for Sher Shah Suri

South-west of Buxar; the exact ground is part of local heritage.

The Panchkoshi Journey

The Buxar country

A five-part sacred circuit that maps Rama’s voyage through the Buxar country — told as five routes, kept alive by a festival and a parikrama.

Local tradition records five sacred paths — panch koshi — associated with the voyage of Lord Rama through this region, and celebrates them through the annual Panchkoshi Mela. Devotees also undertake a Panch Kosi Parikrama of the Buxar region, especially in the Margashirsha month (around November–December), when the countryside around Ahirauli is a noted gathering point. The specific names and route details of each of the five stops are being confirmed from district sources before publication.

When
Annual Panchkoshi Mela · Margashirsha parikrama (Nov–Dec)
Note
Route timings and fares change with the season — confirm them locally

The circuit runs through the Buxar countryside; Ahirauli is a documented stop.
